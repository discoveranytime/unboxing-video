🎥 Evidence guide

How to film an unboxing video correctly

One unbroken recording — sealed box to empty box. Here's how to capture clear proof of what arrived and the shape it arrived in, so any problem is easy to show and easy to resolve.

🎬

Roll camera before you rip

That moment the box lands on your doorstep is exciting — but the smartest minutes you'll spend are the ones with the camera rolling before the tape comes off. This is the full playbook for filming it right, start to finish.

📋 Educational reference only. This page covers general practices for documenting parcel deliveries. It isn't legal advice and isn't connected to any seller or carrier.
01

Why the video matters

Proof beats memory, every single time.

A proper unboxing video proves two things: what showed up, and what shape it was in. Done right, it lays down a clear timeline — the box sealed on camera, the box opened on camera, each item lifted out and shown, and the empty box shown at the end.

That timeline is what turns a dispute into a reviewable record. When something is missing, broken, or leaking, everyone involved can look at the same footage instead of trading guesses.

Without it, everything is a guess. Was the item never packed? Damaged on the road? Counted wrong? Taken after delivery? The video can't answer every question by itself — but it gives any investigation solid ground to stand on. Some sellers won't even open a missing-item case without video evidence, so it's worth knowing what your seller asks for.
02

Before you press record

Set the stage first — there's no pause button on proof.

  • Charged device with plenty of free space — a dead battery mid-unboxing defeats the entire purpose.
  • Bright, even lighting — labels, seals, and damage have to be readable on camera.
  • A clear surface with room to lay items out one by one as you remove them.
  • A wide enough frame for the package and your hands to stay visible together.
  • Somewhere private — avoid spots where other people might wander into the recording.
⛔ The golden rule: the camera rolls while the box is still sealed. Footage that starts after opening has already lost its most valuable seconds.
03

Film the sealed box

Four steps: prove how it arrived — then open it on camera.

1

Show the whole exterior

Before any cuts or tears, film every side of the package: shipping labels, tracking stickers, tape lines, and seals. Pan slowly and give the camera time to focus on each face.

2

Linger on any damage

Crushed corners, dents, tears, punctures, wet patches, broken seals — film each one from two or three angles. This damage happened before the box reached you — capture it before you open anything.

3

Prove the seal is intact

Show the tape is uncut, the seals unbroken, the wrap undisturbed. Rotate the box slowly so every side gets its moment — an unbroken seal on camera is what proves nobody opened it before you.

4

Then open it on camera

Cut the tape and open the box without leaving the frame. Show the inside as first seen — padding, documents, and contents exactly as they sat when the box was opened.

04

One continuous take

No pauses. No restarts. No edits.

Film the entire unboxing as a single unbroken recording. Don't pause it, don't stop and start a second clip, and don't cut sections out afterward.

Here's why that matters: one continuous file proves the items on screen at the beginning are the same items that came out of the box during the recording. Separate clips — or photos taken after everything's already out — can't make that link.

Keep it all in frame. The package and its contents should stay in the camera's view for the whole take, and your hands shouldn't cover up labels or important details while you're showing them.
05

Every item on camera

Slow down — the details are the whole point.

📦 One at a time

Lift each item out individually and hold it up long enough for the camera to lock focus. Make sure the identifying details read clearly:

  • Product name and description
  • Labels, quantities, and lot or batch numbers
  • Packaging condition and any dates printed on it

Small print? Bring the item closer — don't just wave it past the lens and hope for the best.

🔢 Count out loud

Several identical items? Group them together in frame so the number is undeniable — and count aloud while recording: "one, two, three, four." A visible pile plus a spoken count leaves no room for argument.

🔍 Give extra time

Something arrived broken, leaking, opened, or just wrong? Spend extra time on it from several angles before moving on. Don't assume it's "obvious from a distance" — if a detail could matter later, get it close to the lens and make sure it's sharp.

06

Until the box is empty

Don't stop until there's nothing left to film.

Keep recording until every last item is out of the package. Then turn the camera on the inside of the box — shift the padding, inserts, and bags aside so it's obvious nothing is hiding underneath.

This closing shot is what proves the items you showed are everything that was inside. The finished video should tell one clean story: sealed at the start, opened on camera, items shown one by one, empty box at the end.

⛔ Found a problem halfway? Don't stop there. Finish the full unboxing first — there could be a second issue hiding behind the first, and only a complete video proves the whole story.
07

Report problems fast

Quick, factual, and with the footage attached.

If anything is missing, incorrect, damaged, or leaking, contact the seller or their representative as soon as you reasonably can. Keep the message short and factual, and include:

  • What you received and when the package arrived
  • Exactly what appears to be wrong
  • That you filmed the complete unboxing
  • The video and supporting photos
  • Any order or tracking details they need

Wrong item? State clearly what you expected versus what actually arrived. Damage? Describe it precisely instead of saying "it's broken."

"Hi — my parcel arrived today and one of the items I ordered seems to be missing. I filmed the complete unboxing from the sealed box through to the empty box, and I've attached the video and photos. Please let me know what else you need to look into this."

They may need time to check with the warehouse or shipping team and review the footage — give them what they ask for, and hang onto your originals until the issue is fully resolved.

08

Keep the original

The raw file is the evidence. Everything else is a copy.

Don't delete the original recording — and don't trim it down just to make it small enough to send. If the file is too large to attach directly, use a file-sharing link instead of cutting the video.

Keep the whole package together somewhere safe:

  • The original full-length, unedited video
  • Photos of labels, quantities, and any damage
  • Tracking details and shipping information
  • Your order confirmation or invoice
  • Any messages exchanged about the issue
Trimmed clips are fine for sharing — just never let the edited version become the only version you have.

One more thing: make sure the footage is actually usable. A steady camera and good lighting make the difference between proof and mush. Odds are you'll never need the video — but having proof you don't need beats needing proof you don't have.

09

Mistakes that wreck it

Nine ways good footage goes bad.

❌

Rolling after opening

The sealed condition is gone forever — the most valuable seconds are lost.

❌

Drifting out of frame

If the lens never saw it, it might as well never have happened.

❌

Stopping halfway

Every gap in the recording is a gap someone can question.

❌

Pausing or editing

Cuts break the unbroken chain the whole video is built on.

❌

Rushing past items

Blurry fly-bys capture nothing usable — slow down.

❌

Filming too far back

Labels and quantities turn to unreadable mush.

❌

Skipping the count

"A pile of them" proves nothing — group, show, count aloud.

❌

Deleting the original

The raw file is the evidence. Keep it until everything is resolved.

❌

Photos only

Still images can't show the unbroken timeline a video does.

10

Master checklist

Run through this every time. Future-you says thanks.

🎬 Before opening

  • Start recording
  • Show the entire package
  • Show shipping information
  • Show seals and tape
  • Document visible damage
  • Camera stable and focused

📦 During the unboxing

  • Keep the package in frame
  • Open the package on camera
  • Show the inside packaging
  • Remove items individually
  • Show labels clearly
  • Verify quantities
  • Document damage or abnormalities
  • Don't pause or stop recording

🏁 At the end

  • Remove all contents
  • Show the empty package
  • Show all items together
  • Take extra photos if needed
  • Save the original video
  • Keep the original packaging
  • Report problems promptly
